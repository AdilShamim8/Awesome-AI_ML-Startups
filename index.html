01Executive summary

Six takeaways that survive scrutiny

Every claim below is computed from the governed dataset — no headline is quoted that the underlying rows cannot support. Chapter references point to the evidence.

  1. 01

    Capital is compressing into one extraordinary twelve-month window.

    Of the $306.8B in latest-round capital tracked across the registry, 87.3% — $267.9B across 64 rounds — closed in 2026 alone. The trailing twelve months (November 2025 through October 24, 2026) account for $273.4B across 77 rounds. Twenty-one of the thirty $1B-plus rounds on file closed this year. This is not a broad market expansion; it is a surge concentrated in time, and any strategy built on 2024-era assumptions is already stale.

    Evidence · Section 04 · Capital & Momentum
  2. 02

    Value concentrates at the frontier to a degree that reshapes every adjacent decision.

    Generative AI and AI/ML infrastructure — 43 of 118 companies — carry $261.8B of latest-round capital, 85.3% of the total. The five largest rounds account for 74.6% of everything tracked; the top ten, 82.7%. When two categories and ten companies carry a market, category-level "AI market" statistics are arithmetic artifacts, not observations.

    Evidence · Sections 04 & 06 · Capital, Segments
  3. 03

    The cohort is young, and value creation has never been faster.

    The median company on file was founded in 2018. Sixty-six of the 118 were founded in 2021 or later, and they already hold $1.78T in known valuation — 52.2% of the $3.40T total. Anthropic, founded 2021, is carried at $965B. Safe Superintelligence, founded 2023, at $32B. The compounding clock starts earlier than the market's mental model admits.

    Evidence · Section 03 · Landscape
  4. 04

    Geography is still a monoculture — but the October pass cracked it open.

    79.7% of the registry is American; San Francisco alone accounts for 45 of 118 companies. The October 24 research pass added the dataset's first Asia-Pacific rows — DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Zhipu AI, MiniMax and Manus AI in China, Sakana AI in Japan, Sarvam AI in India, LMArena in Singapore — bringing Asia-Pacific to seven companies and the country list to eleven. The outliers are now real rows with primary sources, not aspirations.

    Evidence · Sections 03 & 08 · Landscape, Opportunities
  5. 05

    The exit window is open — and consolidation is setting the default outcome.

    Nine of the 118 companies are acquired or pending acquisition and five are public. The largest exit events on file — xAI merged into SpaceX, Wiz into Google, Scale AI majority-staked by Meta, Brex into Capital One, Anysphere's round closed from SpaceX's war chest — were set in motion by the same capital wave this dataset records. When the round sizes peak, the consolidation clock is already running.

    Evidence · Section 04 · Exits & status events
  6. 06

    The weakest asset in the private markets is the data itself — and that is an opportunity.

    32 of 118 rows carry partial verification; 37 of 193 events are single-source or reported-in-progress. DeepSeek's reported ~$15B round at ~$74B (October 22) is excluded from confirmed totals because it is reported, not closed. Every aggregator faces the same constraint; almost none disclose it. A dataset that flags what it cannot verify is worth more than one that pretends.

02Data foundation

The dataset was the first problem to solve

The registry was rebuilt once, in September 2026, and extended once, on October 24. Two passes, one canonical store, and a validation gate that runs before anything ships — including this page.

The October 24 refresh — what actually changed

The September rebuild left a 47-day hole: nothing between Crusoe's September 3 filing and the month's end. The October pass ran a dedicated research window — September 8 through October 24 — and closed it. Twelve gap-window events went into the ledger, $14.3B of it confirmed: Ramp's $1.85B at a $60B valuation (Oct 15), Mistral's €3B Series D (Sep 8), Cognition's $2B at $48B (Sep 8), Harvey's $550M at $15.5B (Sep 9), Crusoe's $3.9B at $30.9B (Sep 17), Etched's $700M at $21B, TypeSafe's $870M at $7.5B, Moonshot's $2B at $20B, DeepSeek's first external $7.4B at $50B+, Walden's $300M seed, Atoms' $1.7B, and CMR-Stryker's $400M.

The registry itself moved from 103 to 118: fifteen additions (the first Asia-Pacific cohort, plus Etched, TypeSafe, Walden Robotics, Atoms, CMR Surgical, Pigment, EliseAI and Norm Ai), seventeen corrections to existing records (round amounts, stages, statuses, source URLs), and twenty-one new events with three event-level fixes (172 → 193). Every change is written to the evidence log — 36 new blocks on top of the 152 from September — with the query, the source, and the exact claim verified.

What did not happen matters as much: no historical snapshot was edited, no warning was silenced, and no number was rounded into agreement. Where sources conflict — OpenAI's 2026 round talks carried figures from $25B to $30B+ across Reuters, Bloomberg and The Information — the conflict is preserved in the row's notes rather than averaged away.

05The 22-month record

2025 built the runway. 2026 took off.

Every recorded funding event, month by month, with each month's largest round named. Twelve pre-2025 events are retained for round history but excluded from the monthly grid; partial events are counted and marked, never summed.

2025: the base camp year

110 events, $104.3B confirmed. The year's defining rounds were already outliers: OpenAI's $40B (March, at a $300B valuation), Anthropic's $13B (September), Scale AI's $14.3B Meta transaction (June) — a 49% stake, not a conventional round. Beneath them, a steady mid-market: 73 events under $500M each. The foundation was poured, but the gravity had not yet arrived.

2026: the gravity year

71 events through October 24, $262.7B confirmed — 2.5× the capital of 2025 on two-thirds of the events. OpenAI's $122B (March, $852B valuation) and Anthropic's $65B (May, $965B) are the two largest private rounds ever recorded anywhere. xAI's $20B (January) closed inside SpaceX. DeepSeek's first external $7.4B (June) moved the frontier's center of mass toward China — and its reported ~$15B (October 22) is already in the ledger, flagged partial.

The compression is arithmetic, not rhetoric: the median confirmed 2026 event is $600M; the median 2025 event, $243M. When the median event more than doubles in a year, the mid-market is not participating in the same market.

07Problems

What the numbers say is broken

Five structural faults, measured on this dataset, each of which distorts decisions made on top of it.

  1. 01

    Frontier gravity distorts every adjacent market.

    Two companies hold 61% of tracked capital. Benchmark against "the AI market" and every non-frontier round reads as underperformance; benchmark within segment and the picture inverts — application-layer companies clear 34.8x valuation-to-capital against the frontier's 10.1x. The aggregate statistic is the distortion.

    Evidence · Sections 04 & 06
  2. 02

    A geographic monoculture with one zip code at its center.

    79.7% USA, 45 of 118 in San Francisco. Europe's 16 companies — Mistral, Helsing, Black Forest Labs, Synthesia, Lovable — carry $73B in known valuation marks, 2.1% of the tracked total. Asia-Pacific's seven companies arrived only in the October pass. The dataset is finally global; the capital is not.

    Evidence · Section 03 · Landscape
  3. 03

    Private-market data is opaque, stale and unverifiable by default.

    37 of 193 events are partial. DeepSeek's ~$15B October round is reported, not closed — excluded here, counted elsewhere. Announcement dates masquerade as close dates in most public datasets; this one records both. The industry's most-cited numbers are its least-verified.

    Evidence · Section 02 · Data Foundation
  4. 04

    The early stage has gone dark.

    10 of 118 companies sit at Seed or Pre-Seed, holding $2.3B — 0.8% of tracked capital. The seed world does not announce; it does not file; it does not appear here. Any "state of AI startups" claim built on visible data is a claim about the late stage wearing a seed-stage costume.

    Evidence · Section 03 · Stage ladder
  5. 05

    Consolidation is setting the default exit before the default company.

    Nine acquisitions or pending exits, one majority stake, five IPOs — against a backdrop where the acquirers (Google, Meta, SpaceX, Capital One, Stryker) are older and larger than the entire registry combined. The modal outcome for an AI company is no longer independence; it is absorption.

    Evidence · Section 04 · Exits
Read the faults as specs

Each fault above is a measurement of this file, and each has a mirror-image specification: report within-segment, not aggregate. Weight geography explicitly. Separate announced from closed. Mark the seed blind spot on every chart. Name the acquirer concentration. The fixes cost nothing but discipline.

What this file cannot fix

Selection bias: companies enter when they raise or get covered. Survivorship: the registry records the living and the acquired, not the dead. Valuation staleness: 22 rows carry no current mark. October is a 24-day month in this release.

08Opportunities

Where the next companies come from

Five openings the data exposes — each with the numbers that make it an opening rather than an opinion.

  1. 01

    The infrastructure compounding core.

    42 companies, $519B known valuation, and the deepest bench of repeat raisers — inference clouds and data platforms that re-price every quarter. Compute demand is the one variable every segment in this file depends on. The layer directly beneath the frontier compounds fastest with the least headline risk.

    Evidence · Section 06 · Infrastructure & DevTools
  2. 02

    Applications: capital efficiency as the wedge.

    34.8x valuation-to-capital — triple the frontier's multiple — on $10.6B raised. Glean, OpenEvidence, Perplexity and ElevenLabs each cleared 44–48x on their last rounds. The application layer is where small rounds buy large marks, because distribution, not capex, is the constraint.

    Evidence · Sections 04 & 06
  3. 03

    Physical AI — capital efficiency beyond the frontier's.

    20 companies turned $20.2B into $211B of known valuation at frontier-grade multiples (10.4x) with revenue contracts (defense, logistics, humanoid labor) rather than API growth alone. Figure at $39B and Anduril at $61B anchor a segment where the ceiling has not been tested.

    Evidence · Section 06 · Physical AI & Defense
  4. 04

    The international arbitrage is now measurable.

    The October pass proved the gap was data, not reality: seven Asia-Pacific companies entered with primary sources in one research window. Europe's 16 companies run on local capital with $73B in known marks. DeepSeek's $50B+ and Moonshot's $20B say the frontier is no longer one country's asset — but 95 of 118 rows are still North American, so the mispricing runs in both directions.

    Evidence · Sections 02–03 · October refresh, geography
  5. 05

    Market intelligence as a product — the meta-opportunity.

    This dataset exists because 47 days of disciplined gap-filling produced 15 companies, 21 events and 17 corrections that no public aggregator had. Verified, dated, source-linked private-market data is scarce enough that building it is a business — the evidence log is the prototype.

    Evidence · Section 02 · The refresh ledger
The pattern underneath

Every opening shares one shape: a place where the aggregate statistic lies. The frontier distorts the average; geography distorts the census; announcement dates distort the timeline; the seed blind spot distorts the stage mix. Each distortion is a mispricing — and a mispricing is a business.

October's own example

The 47-day research window found $14.3B of confirmed capital that the September file missed — 3.9% of the entire dataset's total, in 6% of its calendar span. The gap between "the market" and "the record" is the cheapest research budget available.

09Founder's playbook

What the data says a new founder should do

Not motivation — positioning. Each move below is the contrarian read of a chart in Sections 03–06.

  1. 01

    Pick the stage where proof, not pitch, is priced.

    The application layer clears 34.8x valuation-to-capital; the frontier clears 10.1x on rounds a founder cannot raise. Unless you own a compute moat, the arithmetic says: sell outcomes, not infrastructure, and let the multiple work for you.

    Evidence · Section 06 · Segment multiples
  2. 02

    Go where the census is thin, not where the headlines are.

    EdTech holds 4 companies and $0.1B; HealthTech 7 and $1.0B; Cybersecurity 6 and $2.1B — against 19 frontier companies at $228.3B. Thin census means thin competition for the category's investors, and the October pass showed the gap is data, not demand.

    Evidence · Section 03 · Category charts
  3. 03

    Raise from the investors who actually did the rounds.

    The league table is short and repetitive: a16z (27 registry companies), Sequoia (24), Nvidia (16), Y Combinator (11), Kleiner Perkins and Accel (10 each). These firms priced 2026. A syndicate that has not closed an AI round since 2024 will price your round like 2024.

    Evidence · Section 06 · Investor league table
  4. 04

    Time the raise to the market's actual heartbeat.

    Confirmed capital spikes in March ($125.9B), May ($74.2B) and September ($10.6B) 2026; January opened with $23.8B. The big checks cluster after landmark rounds close, when allocators rebalance. File your round into the echo, not the silence.

    Evidence · Section 04 · Monthly chart
  5. 05

    If you start today: the short version.

    Application-layer or physical-AI vertical; Series A–B proof metrics; a thin-census category with regulated workflows; one league-table investor on the cap table; and a raise filed within 90 days of the next landmark round. The dataset's 2023 founding cohort — 21 companies, $463B already marked — is the existence proof.

    Evidence · Sections 03, 06 · Cohorts, segments

10Recommendations

Decision-ready priorities

Three audiences, three moves each — every number traceable to a section above.

For founders

1 · Price on the segment, not the aggregate. Application multiples run 3× the frontier's; a frontier-anchored comp will undersell an application business.

2 · Build where the census is thin. HealthTech, EdTech, Cybersecurity hold 17 companies combined — categories with demand and without a crowded registry bench.

3 · File into the echo. Landmark rounds pull allocators off the bench within one or two quarters (Section 04's spikes).

For investors

1 · Underwrite concentration honestly. Two rounds are 61% of tracked capital; portfolio construction against "the AI market" is really construction against two names and a residue.

2 · The international window is open and unbid. Seven Asia-Pacific companies entered the file in one 47-day pass; the research cost is a rounding error against the stakes.

3 · Demand the verification layer. 37 partial events and one unverified row are disclosed here; most datasets bury theirs. Price the difference.

For data & ops leaders

1 · Separate announced from closed. This file records both dates and still finds gaps; a pipeline that merges them is lying to someone.

2 · Flag, never delete. The 32 partial rows are the honest 27% of the file — removing them would flatter every statistic and corrupt every trend.

3 · Regenerate, never hand-type. Every derived artifact here — category pages, snapshots, statistics — is rebuilt from one master by scripts, gated by validation and CI. The process is the product.

The one-line version

A market where two companies hold 61% of the capital, the median 2026 round is $600M, and the honest dataset is the scarce asset — build where the census is thin, price on the segment, and treat verification as the product.
